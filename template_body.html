Indicators

Is Bitcoin cheap or expensive right now?

%%NSIG%% indicators rate the market from −1 (historically expensive) to +1 (historically cheap). The ones that worked better in the past count for more; the two that count most are holders' profit and the 4-year average.

%%SIGNALS%%

Where are we in the 4-year cycle?

About every 4 years there is a halving: the reward for the people “mining” Bitcoin is cut in half. Historically, prices rose for about 1.5 years after it, then fell.

Events

What was happening in the world when the price jumped?

We have collected %%NEVENTS%% events: major decisions (e.g. bans in China, the collapse of the FTX exchange, approval of ETFs), scheduled dates (US central bank meetings, elections), exchange hacks and sudden crashes on world markets that are detected automatically. For every big price move we record what was going on in the world at the time (including news headlines and a log of world events), so we have a dense timeline to search for repeating patterns.

World

Mood, patterns and the search for links

Interest in Bitcoin across countries, moves by big players, a test of whether the market has any repeatable patterns, and a sweep through hundreds of indicators from around the world.

%%REGTILES%%

Interest in Bitcoin across countries (last 30 days, compared with last year)

We count how many people read the “Bitcoin” article on Wikipedia in each language. When interest rises, the price usually rises too (and the other way round).

%%REGROWS%%

Stocks and bonds: what Bitcoin moves with

We track every company in the S&P 500, the companies in 13 major world indices (Germany, France, the UK, the Netherlands, Spain, Switzerland, the eurozone, Hong Kong, Korea, India, Canada, Australia, Brazil), more than 100 funds (country markets, sectors, bonds, commodities, currencies) and the indices of about 40 stock exchanges. Across the whole system we follow %%NSERIES%% data series. Stocks and bonds are often the first to react to what is happening in the world, so we look at how the market behaves “under the hood” and what Bitcoin moves together with.

%%MARKETS%%

Liquidity, crypto “behind the scenes” and wild cards

Extra signals from outside the exchanges: how many dollars are flowing through the system (Fed and US Treasury data), how fast stablecoins are growing, whether bitcoins are leaving exchanges, how hedge funds are positioned in other markets (CFTC), what programmers are writing about (Hacker News), what people read on Wikipedia (about 130 articles, from Powell to tulip mania), and even space weather, Mercury retrograde, Friday the 13th, the World Cup and Ramadan. The last few are deliberate “wild cards”: most of them probably mean nothing, but the scanner tests them just as strictly as everything else, so we will see it in the data rather than in gut feelings.

%%EXTRA%%

Money flowing into Bitcoin ETFs, purchases and sales by Strategy (the largest corporate holder of BTC), the bets of the best and worst traders on the Hyperliquid exchange, where every position is public, and the wallets of governments (the US, Germany and El Salvador), checked address by address on the blockchain. Methods

Which forecasting methods actually work?

We tried several methods. We tested each one on past data as if it had been used in real time, and compared it with the simplest assumption: “the price will stay the same”. A method only makes it into the forecast if it clearly beats that. We measure price swings in three complementary ways (%%VOLW%%).

Virtual portfolio

Does active management beat regular buying?

An experiment with virtual money: 10,000 PLN to start and 10,000 PLN every month. About every 6 hours a virtual trader looks at the current price and decides whether to buy, sell or wait, and for how much. It explains every move in a log and learns from the results. The rules are public and fixed in advance. No real trades; this is not investment advice.

Simulator

What if you invested regularly?

Enter your own amounts. We will show how such a plan would have done in Bitcoin's real history and in some example future scenarios. It is a calculator for looking at numbers, not an adviser.

Important. This is an educational simulator, not investment advice or a recommendation. We are not a licensed financial adviser, we do not know your situation and we do not make any trades. Nobody knows Bitcoin's future price, and the last 15 years of history do not have to repeat. Bitcoin has lost 80% of its value before. Do not invest money you could not afford to lose, and talk to a licensed adviser before any important decision.
